## Part A — OFFICIAL facts (what the texts say)

### A1. Mathématiques (NS MENE2623228N, S1; confirmed by S4, S5, S8, S9)
- The exam assesses the knowledge and skills expected at the end of cycle 4, as set out in the 3e programme if it is in force, or otherwise in the cycle 4 programme. For 2027 that means the current cycle 4 programme (see L3).
- The six competencies named in the text: **chercher, modéliser, représenter, raisonner, calculer, communiquer**.
- Exercises must be independent of one another.
- **Partie 1 – automatismes et applications directes (6 pts, 20 min, no calculator).** Short-answer questions. Each one calls on a cycle-4 automatisme, or on a direct application of a property, formula or theorem. No justification is asked for or marked.
  - S8 (inspection) adds:
    - questions are worth 0.5 or 1 pt;
    - the format is a direct answer, true/false, or a QCM with one correct answer out of 4;
    - a wrong answer or no answer costs no points;
    - the number of questions is not fixed.
- **Partie 2 – raisonnement et résolution de problèmes (14 pts, 1 h 40, with calculator).**
  - Any format except QCM.
  - Contexts may come from everyday life or other subjects.
  - Every answer must be justified unless the question says otherwise. Attempts are credited.
  - **2 pts** are given for the clarity and precision of reasoning and for maîtrise de la langue (four dimensions: orthographe, syntaxe, lexique, organisation).
  - In the reference subjects, the questions printed in bold are the ones that assess language.
- The reference subjects (S4, S5) label every item with:
  - the programme domain;
  - the "attendus de fin de cycle";
  - the "notions du programme à mobiliser";
  - the "principales compétences mobilisées".

  This is the official model for how we should tag our own items.

### A2. Sciences (S1)
- 1 h: two disciplines out of PC / SVT / technologie, about 30 min each, written on one answer sheet. Marked /20, with 10 pts per discipline.
- The disciplines are announced on Éduscol two months before the exam.
- **2 pts out of 20** go to maîtrise de la langue, 1 per discipline.
- The subject assesses the candidate's ability to:
  1. master the knowledge and skills in the programmes;
  2. **exploit numerical and/or experimental data**;
  3. analyse and understand information using each discipline's reasoning, methods and models;
  4. write correctly, clearly and precisely to explain their reasoning.
- Some exercises require **prise d'initiative**: an open question that asks the candidate to search and reason. Partial approaches are credited.

**Reading of the trend (analysis):**
1. The new Partie 2 is shorter. It has about 4 exercises worth 2–4.5 pts each, against 5 exercises of about 20/100 before.
2. Geometry (Pythagore + Thalès, often combined in one exercise) and functions/modelling are now the backbone of Partie 2.
3. Probability, statistics, arithmetic and transformations have moved largely into Partie 1.
4. Scratch is still present in most papers.

**Design implications (analysis):**
- Mix three answer formats: direct numeric/short answers, 4-choice QCM, and vrai/faux.
- Make some distractors classic errors, such as units of area vs length, a sign error, or a percentage applied the wrong way.
- Calibrate to about 2 minutes per question.
- No calculator, so keep numbers mental-friendly (multiples of 10, small fractions, simple decimals).

### B4. Reasoning expectations (Partie 2)
These come from S1, S4, S5 and the old-format "indications". They are official in substance; the phrasing here is ours.
- Justify every answer. Name the theorem and check its hypotheses: right angle for Pythagore, alignment and parallel lines for Thalès. The converse of Pythagore, and Thalès or its contrapositive, are explicit targets.
- Answer the question in a sentence. 2/14 points go to rédaction, so practice items should ask for an explicit concluding sentence and a minimum level of written explanation on "bold" questions.
- Give a value rounded "au dixième / au centième / à l'unité" as asked, with units.
- Show partial work, which is credited.
- Algebraic proof "quel que soit le nombre": develop and reduce both expressions and compare.
- Interpret a statistic in context (Métropole 2026 Ex1 Q4b type).
